II —

The docket

Most platforms wrap their copy with the same few tricks — AES encryption, HTML pages, SVG layers. Folio’s reader already handles each of those building blocks in-browser, so the rest of the shelf is a matter of wiring one backend after another.

  • Zanichelli — Kitaboo + BookTab backends
  • Pearson eText — device registration + RSA/AES
  • Sanoma · My Digital Book — SVG page books
  • Scuolabook, MEE2, Hoepli demo
  • Educadhoc (FR, Hachette) · WSET (UK, Kitaboo)
  • digi4school / scook (AT) · German publishers (Cornelsen, Klett, Westermann)
  • Oxford Learner’s Bookshelf (UK)
  • NCERT (IN, free official PDFs) · McGraw-Hill, RedShelf (US)

No downloader here is a mystery: they all share the same four building blocks — fetch pages, unlock, render, merge — and Folio does each of those four steps inside a browser tab.

III —

Keep it small, keep it yours

Nothing to install

No app, no account with us, no waiting room. The Reader works on any modern desktop browser.

Keys stay local

Your token and password live in a plaintext Cabinet on your own device — readable by you, kept out of every request except the byte-for-byte relay to the platform you’re already logged into. Connect a platform and its shelf publishes to the catalog as metadata; press Download (one book or the whole shelf) and the PDFs assemble in the background, one after another.

Nothing to pay

Free hosting, free bandwidth, open source. The site is a static page; the extra relay is a standard free Cloudflare tier.

How the Cabinet works
There is nothing to unlock. Your accounts, tokens and shelf sit in one plaintext JSON store on this device, mirrored to your own Cloudflare D1 if you deploy the site — open the page and the desk is right there. Nothing is encrypted or hidden from you: check it yourself in DevTools. What never leaves the device is anything you didn’t publish — and every shelf and PDF you do publish goes to the public catalog anonymously, always.
